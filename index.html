🧮 Pharmaceutical Calculations

1. Dilution

Formula: C₁V₁ = C₂V₂

Example: Prepare 100 mL of 5% solution from a 20% stock.

20 × V₁ = 5 × 100

V₁ = 500 ÷ 20 = 25 mL

Measure 25 mL of the 20% stock and add vehicle to a final volume of 100 mL.

2. Percentage Strength

A 5% w/v preparation contains 5 g of solute in every 100 mL of final preparation.

For 500 mL:

5 g × 500/100 = 25 g

3. Ratio Strength

A 1 in 1000 preparation means 1 part of substance is contained in 1000 parts of preparation.

4. Alligation

Alligation is used when two preparations of different strengths are mixed to obtain an intermediate strength.

Preparation Strength Difference from desired
Lower 5% 20 − 10 = 10
Desired 10%
Higher 20% 10 − 5 = 5

Ratio of lower : higher = 10 : 5 = 2 : 1.

5. Dose Calculation

Required quantity = Required dose ÷ Stock dose × Quantity supplied

Example: A patient requires 250 mg. The available preparation contains 125 mg in 5 mL.

250 ÷ 125 × 5 = 10 mL.

Practice Questions

  1. Prepare 250 mL of a 2% solution from a 10% stock solution.
  2. How many grams are required for 750 mL of a 4% w/v preparation?
  3. What does 1 in 1000 ratio strength mean?
  4. Use alligation to prepare 15% from 10% and 25% preparations.
  5. A preparation contains 500 mg in 10 mL. What volume provides 750 mg?
  1. 50 mL of the 10% stock; dilute to 250 mL.
  2. 30 g.
  3. 1 part solute in 1000 parts final preparation.
  4. Lower:higher = 10:5 = 2:1.
  5. 15 mL.
