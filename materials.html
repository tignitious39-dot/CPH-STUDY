📚 Core CPH Materials

💊 Pharmacology

Study drugs, their actions, uses, adverse effects and important interactions.

What is Pharmacology?

Pharmacology is the science concerned with drugs and their interactions with living systems.

Major Areas

  • Pharmacodynamics – what the drug does to the body.
  • Pharmacokinetics – what the body does to the drug.
  • Clinical pharmacology – application of drugs in patients.
  • Pharmacotherapeutics – use of drugs to prevent and treat disease.
Remember: Pharmacokinetics can be summarized as ADME — Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism and Excretion.

Drug Administration Routes

  • Oral
  • Intravenous
  • Intramuscular
  • Subcutaneous
  • Topical
  • Inhalational
Quick Check: What are the four major processes represented by ADME?

🧮 Pharmaceutical Calculations

Master the calculations commonly encountered in pharmacy practice and examinations.

1. Percentage Strength

Percentage strength expresses the amount of active ingredient in 100 parts of the preparation.

% strength = amount of solute / amount of preparation × 100

2. Dilution

C₁V₁ = C₂V₂

Where C₁ is the initial concentration, V₁ is the volume taken, C₂ is the required concentration and V₂ is the final volume.

3. Ratio and Proportion

A ratio compares two quantities. Cross multiplication can be used to solve many pharmaceutical proportion problems.

4. Allegation

Allegation is a method used to determine the quantities of two different strengths required to prepare a mixture of an intermediate strength.

Example concept: To prepare a strength between a high-strength preparation and a low-strength preparation, the differences between each strength and the desired strength determine the mixing ratio.

5. Dose Calculations

Volume required = Dose required / Dose available × Volume containing available dose
Practice:
A patient requires 250 mg of a medicine. The available preparation contains 500 mg in 5 mL. What volume should be administered?

🦠 Microbiology

Learn microorganisms, infection, sterilization and antimicrobial principles.

Microbiology

Microbiology is the study of microorganisms including bacteria, viruses, fungi and protozoa.

Important Topics

  • Bacterial structure
  • Gram staining
  • Bacterial growth
  • Sterilization
  • Disinfection
  • Antimicrobial agents
  • Infection control

Gram Staining

Gram staining differentiates bacteria according to differences in their cell-wall structure.

Exam Tip: Know the basic differences between Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, including cell-wall structure and staining reaction.

🩺 Clinical Pharmacy

Focus on safe and effective use of medicines in patient care.

Clinical Pharmacy

Clinical pharmacy focuses on optimizing medication therapy and supporting safe, effective and appropriate medicine use.

Key Areas

  • Medication history
  • Patient counselling
  • Adverse drug reactions
  • Drug interactions
  • Medication adherence
  • Patient monitoring
Patient Safety: Always verify the patient, medicine, dose, route and relevant instructions before medicine administration.

📖 CPH Revision

Use structured revision to identify weak areas and prepare for examinations.

Effective Revision Cycle

  1. Learn the concept.
  2. Make short notes.
  3. Test yourself without looking at the answer.
  4. Mark your work.
  5. Review mistakes.
  6. Repeat difficult topics.

High-Yield Revision

  • Definitions
  • Classifications
  • Mechanisms
  • Indications
  • Contraindications
  • Adverse effects
  • Calculations
  • Short-answer questions
  • MCQs
Study Principle: Active recall and spaced revision are generally more effective for long-term retention than repeatedly reading the same notes.
